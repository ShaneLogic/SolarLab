Steady state  ·  time domain  ·  same semiconductor equations

Two engines, one device physics

SCAPS-1D and SolarLab solve the same coupled Poisson + continuity system for a thin-film stack. They differ in three decisions that propagate through everything else: which variables are unknown, whether time is a coordinate, and how light gets into the film. This page puts those decisions side by side and shows what each one buys.

Companion to the SolarLab solver walkthrough. Everything on the amber side is sourced from the SCAPS manual (v3.3.03, February 2016) and the Burgelman group's published algorithm papers; everything on the cyan side is sourced from the SolarLab tree.

axis · unknowns

What the solver treats as unknown

Both codes discretise Poisson's equation plus two carrier-continuity equations. The choice of dependent variable is the first fork in the road: SCAPS carries the quasi-Fermi levels, SolarLab carries the densities and adds a third transported species.

SCAPS-1D

(ψ, EFn, EFp) — steady state

— Poisson —
−∂/∂x( ε ∂ψ/∂x ) = q( p − n + ND+ − NA− + ρdef )

— continuity (∂/∂t = 0) —
∂Jn/∂x = q( Un − G )
∂Jp/∂x = −q( Up − G )

— constitutive —
Jn = μn n ∂EFn/∂x    Jp = μp p ∂EFp/∂x
  • Current is written as a quasi-Fermi-level gradient, so the effective-DOS and band-offset terms are inside the variable itself.
  • Boltzmann statistics throughout; Fermi–Dirac and band-gap narrowing are not implemented.
  • Defect charge ρdef is rich: mono-, di- and multivalent, amphoteric, single-level / uniform / Gauss / band-tail distributions, plus metastable configurations solved by their own occupancy iteration.
  • Time appears only through a separate small-signal linearisation, Y = δJ/δuext = jωC + G.

SolarLab

y = (n, p, P) per node — time-resolved

— transported (method of lines) —
∂n/∂t = (1/q) ∂Jn/∂x + G − R
∂p/∂t = −(1/q) ∂Jp/∂x + G − R
∂P/∂t = ∂/∂x[ DI( ∂P/∂x + (P/VT) ∂φ/∂x ) ]

— solved, not integrated —
−∂/∂x( ε ∂φ/∂x ) = q( p − n + P − NA + ND )
  • Carrier densities are the state; φ is recovered by a prefactored tridiagonal solve once per right-hand-side evaluation — no inner Poisson–Newton loop.
  • A third species, the mobile ionic vacancy P, drifts in the same field (Nernst–Planck, optional second species, optional steric crowding limit).
  • Because the SG flux drives carriers with φ+χ and φ+χ+Eg, the effective-DOS term SCAPS gets for free must be folded in explicitly — dos_band_potentials, worth 137 mV on the parity stack.
  • Fermi–Dirac is available per layer but deliberately off by default: the reference codes are Boltzmann.
delta Quasi-Fermi variables are the better-conditioned choice for a pure steady state — that is why SCAPS converges on stacks where a density-based Newton stalls. Densities are the necessary choice once you want ∂/∂t ≠ 0, because ion transport, trapping and displacement current are all statements about how a density changes in time.
axis · discretisation

Where the grid points go

Both codes cluster nodes where the bands bend. The difference is that SCAPS re-decides the grid during the solve, and doubles every interface node — a choice with real physical consequences at heterojunctions.

SCAPS-1D

adaptive, re-generated between iterations

  • Coarse in the bulk, fine at interfaces and contacts.
  • Two mesh points share each interface coordinate — one belonging to each adjacent layer — so interface carrier densities are genuine per-side plane quantities.
  • Optional mesh recalculation at every iteration: adjacent-node ratios of exp(qψ/kT), exp(EFn/kT), exp(EFp/kT), R and G are tested against fmax = 1.60 (≈ 12 mV) to insert points and fmin = 1.05 (≈ 1.2 mV) to remove them.
  • Up to 7 semiconductor layers; up to 10 mesh adaptions per voltage.

SolarLab

static tanh-clustered multilayer grid

  • Per-layer node counts with tanh clustering toward every interface, built once and reused for the whole run.
  • One node per interface, shared by both layers — which is why the band offset lands inside a single cell and has to be handled by a thermionic-emission cap on the Scharfetter–Gummel flux.
  • The fixed grid is what makes the MaterialArrays cache and the prefactored Poisson LU possible: geometry-dependent work happens once, not per iteration.
  • Interface plane densities, when needed, are reconstructed physically rather than meshed: a quasi-steady 2×2 flux balance (interface_plane_closure) or explicit plane-state unknowns in the steady-state Newton.
delta SCAPS trades per-iteration cost for robustness on steep gradings and multivalent defects. SolarLab trades adaptivity for a cache that survives ~105 right-hand-side evaluations per sweep — the correct trade when the same geometry is integrated through thousands of time steps rather than solved a few hundred times. The doubled interface node is the one place where SCAPS's grid is physically richer, and closing that gap took SolarLab five separate formulations.
axis · driver

How a solution is actually reached

This is the deepest split. SCAPS is a nonlinear-system solver wrapped in a continuation strategy. SolarLab is, by default, a stiff initial-value integrator — with a direct steady-state Newton driver added later precisely so that SCAPS-comparable quantities could be defined.

SCAPS-1D

Gummel iteration + Newton–Raphson substeps

— continuation ladder —
startpoint (flat EF, no potential drop)
  ↓  dark equilibrium, 0 V
  ↓  short circuit, 1 sun, 0 V
  ↓  working point
  ↓  first measurement point → sweep
  • Decoupled Gummel outer loop, Newton–Raphson inner substeps on each equation.
  • Updates are clamped at 0.5 kT/q per iteration (ψ, EFn, EFp); convergence when every update falls below 0.001 kT; up to 1000 iterations.
  • Automatic intermediate substeps between rungs when two situations are too far apart — the standard cure for divergence.
  • Optional "stop after Voc"; up to 201 measurement points per sweep.

SolarLab

method of lines + implicit Radau (and an SS Newton)

— per voltage step —
y(tk) → solve_ivp(Radau IIA, max_step capped)
  ↳ on failure: bisect settle time (≤ 10×)
  ↳ last resort: BDF retry
y(tk+1) carries n, p and the ion profile forward

— optional ion-free steady state —
F(y) = 0 by damped log-space Newton, LU reused
  • Radau IIA (L-stable, 5th order) absorbs the stiffness of the ion–carrier–field coupling; the max-step cap exists because near flat band the Jacobian is near-singular and the error estimator under-reports.
  • The steady-state driver runs on the same assembled right-hand side — one physics, two drivers — with a certified transient fallback wherever Newton cannot finish, so nothing is silently faked.
  • Ionic memory across bias steps is not post-processing: the previous converged state is the initial condition.
delta A steady-state solver answers "what does this device do at V?". A time integrator answers "what does this device do while V changes at rate r?". Perovskites are the case where those two questions have measurably different answers, and where the second one is what the experiment recorded.
axis · boundaries

How the contacts are held

SCAPS-1D

metal work function, or computed flat band

  • Front and back contacts take a metal work function Φm for majority carriers, or a "flat bands" option in which SCAPS back-solves Φm at each temperature so the bands are flat — post-2014 by solving total-charge neutrality including deep defects.
  • Finite surface-recombination velocities for electrons and holes set the contact kinetics (107 cm/s is the conventional default).
  • Optional tunnelling to and from the contact; wavelength-dependent reflection/transmission filter files per contact.
  • External series and shunt resistance are applied after the simulation by re-mapping the terminal voltage — they are not inside the transport problem (and are excluded from QE).

SolarLab

three selectable conventions

  • Ohmic Dirichlet pin (default, IonMonger convention): boundary densities clamped to the dark doping equilibrium, Poisson referenced to the configured Vbi.
  • Robin / selective / Schottky: finite Sn, Sp per carrier per side, J = ±q·S·(density − densityeq); S → ∞ recovers ohmic, S = 0 is perfectly blocking.
  • Flat-band metal contacts — the SCAPS convention — with the Poisson boundary driven by the computed work-function difference compute_V_bi() instead of a frozen input.
  • Documented limit: flat-band contacts plus the steady-state Newton is a known incompatible pair (Newton stalls on the Robin + steep-Vbi conditioning). It is recorded as an engine boundary, not patched over.
delta Contact convention is the single largest source of apparent disagreement between the two codes on doping sweeps: a bias-dependent, doping-swung Vbi boundary responds to ETL doping quite differently from SCAPS's fixed metal work function. SolarLab now implements both conventions — which is what turned a "wrong trend" into a named, reproducible boundary condition difference.
axis · heterojunctions

What happens at an interface

Both codes model interface transport by thermionic emission and interface recombination by an SRH-type cycle. The formulations differ in what carrier densities the recombination rate is allowed to see.

SCAPS-1D

thermionic emission on doubled nodes

  • Interface transport is thermionic emission with the thermal velocity taken as the smaller of the two adjacent layers; a small quasi-Fermi-level step therefore exists at every interface, even with zero band offset.
  • Interface recombination follows Pauwels–Vanhoutte theory — an SRH extension in which one trap occupancy balances capture from both sides.
  • Four semiclassical tunnelling channels: band-to-band, intraband, band ↔ interface defect, band ↔ contact. All are DC-only; the AC problem does not see them.

SolarLab

TE cap on the SG flux + plane-density closure

  • Where |ΔEC| or |ΔEV| exceeds 0.05 eV the Scharfetter–Gummel flux is capped at the Richardson–Dushman thermionic limit, preventing the single-cell offset from over-injecting.
  • Interface SRH uses per-interface (vn, vp). Because the interface is one shared node, plane densities are reconstructed: a per-RHS 2×2 implicit flux balance, or explicit interface-plane state unknowns inside the steady-state Newton.
  • Tunnelling is a single channel — a static Padovani–Stratton thermionic-field-emission enhancement Γ ≥ 1 folded into the Richardson constant, deliberately build-time so it cannot perturb Newton contraction.
delta SCAPS is ahead here, and the record says so plainly: three separate rate-algebra reformulations on bulk-node densities were built and falsified against SCAPS's interface trends before the plane-density route worked. The lesson that came out of it is reusable — any density-product rate law evaluated on quasi-Fermi-consistent bulk nodes is blind to the trap level in this regime; only true plane densities carry the response.
axis · optics

How light becomes G(x)

SCAPS's internal optical model is deliberately simple — incoherent Beer–Lambert absorption, with reflection allowed only at the contacts and no reflections at internal interfaces. Absorption comes from a file or from the model α(λ) = (A + B/hν)·√(hν − Eg). Anything better is expected to arrive as an external g(x) file. SolarLab computes the optics itself, coherently.

SCAPS-1D

incoherent, single pass

  • Beer–Lambert through the stack; α from a model or a file; α(λ,y) can be graded with composition.
  • Reflection/transmission filters at the contacts only, neutral-density and cut-off filters, illumination from either side, a library of spectra (AM0, AM1.5D, AM1.5G, monochromatic).
  • No interference, no interface reflection, no back-reflector standing wave — the manual states this explicitly and offers the g(x) import as the escape hatch.

SolarLab

coherent TMM, closed optical loop

  • Pettersson/Burkhard transfer matrix over the full stack including an optical-only substrate, with the nreal/nambient Poynting correction that makes R+T+A = 1 exactly.
  • Photon recycling: band-edge escape probability Pesc = min(1, 1/4n²αd) rescales the radiative coefficient of layers tagged as absorbers.
  • Self-consistent reabsorption: the trapped fraction is returned as a generation source per RHS call, so emission from a high-np region can feed a low-np region.
  • One TMM over a full tandem stack partitions G(x) into sub-cells, junction layers counted as parasitic absorption.
delta This is the cleanest capability gap on the page. Thin-film perovskite cells with a metal rear electrode are optical cavities; where the interference maxima sit relative to the junction changes Jsc and the shape of the EQE. In SCAPS that physics has to be imported; in SolarLab it is computed from the same layer stack that carries the transport parameters, so an optical and an electrical change stay consistent by construction.

axis · consequence

The measurement a steady-state engine cannot produce

Hysteresis is not a numerical artefact to be removed — it is the observable that carries the ionic time constants. A steady-state solver returns the single quasi-static curve: the limit the scan approaches as the rate goes to zero. Drag the scan rate below and watch the two families meet it.

axis · contribution

Where SolarLab is genuinely new

Stated narrowly, so each claim is checkable against the tree.

01Ion–carrier coupling in real time

Ions are a transported species in the same Poisson field as the carriers, integrated by an L-stable implicit method. Hysteresis, TPV, degradation and large-signal impedance all fall out of one formulation instead of being separate models.

02Optics computed, not imported

Coherent TMM over the same layer stack that carries the transport parameters, closed by photon recycling and self-consistent reabsorption. The cavity that a real thin-film cell is shows up in G(x) automatically.

03Lateral dimension

A 2D solver with sparse Poisson and 2D Scharfetter–Gummel, validated bit-identical against 1D in the lateral-uniform limit, then used for what 1D cannot express: grain boundaries with their own lifetimes, and Voc as a function of grain size.

04Monolithic tandems

One transfer matrix over top + junction + bottom partitions the generation; the sub-cells are swept independently and series-matched on a common current grid. Junction layers are accounted as parasitic absorption rather than ignored.

05Two drivers, one physics

The steady-state Newton and the transient integrator share the same assembled right-hand side. A quantity can be defined the SCAPS way (ion-free steady state) or the experimental way (finite scan rate) without maintaining two physics models that drift apart.

06Reproducibility as infrastructure

Device stacks are immutable dataclasses built from YAML; every physics upgrade sits behind a flag with a bit-identical default; regression gates pin Voc/Jsc/FF envelopes; an automated parity loop scores the model against reference data and records refuted hypotheses so they are not retried.

axis · calibration

Where SCAPS is stronger, and what parity actually looks like

A comparison that only flatters one side is not usable in a thesis defence. SCAPS remains the stronger tool on several axes, and the SolarLab–SCAPS agreement has documented limits.

▸Defect physics

Multivalent and amphoteric states, Gaussian and band-tail distributions, optical excitation of defects (IPV), metastable configurations with their own occupancy iteration. SolarLab has none of that depth.

▸Adaptive meshing

Re-gridding between iterations on the exp(qψ/kT) ratio is why SCAPS handles steep grading and deep-defect occupancy where a fixed grid needs manual node counts.

▸AC analysis

An exact small-signal linearisation is both faster and cleaner than demodulating an integrated waveform — the right tool for C–V and C–f.

▸Maturity

Two decades of use, a large published-parameter ecosystem, curve fitting, batch/recorder workflows and a scripting language. It is the reference the field reads results in.
